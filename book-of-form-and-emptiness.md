## Thoughts/Description

A very clever book. The first book I have noted and logged in a very long time. The book being one of the primary characters was an excellent aspect.

The boy being Kannon was a wonderful thing I understood quite well from the beginning. His description of egodeath was quite surreal. 

The only character I truly disliked was the therapist. Felt to me like a lot of what is wrong with current mental health institutions. But this has probably always been the case. Trying to diagnose people who are unable to live in the difficult conditions of life we have created today. Do they truly not understand what is going on? There is a genuine innocence to that character, just enough of her character built out so that I see where she is coming from, but still. 

The mom’s hoarding issue was quite anxiety inducing. 

Really beautiful character development with our main cast. 

Excellent interweaving of buddhist ideas. Consumerism, simplicity, hungry ghosts, the rat race, that all objects contain consciousness and want to be happy and free.
